Lesson 0001 — Fail-closed gates

Tied to mission: closing the AI/software gap. Primary source: your own Helix Codex constitution + OWASP "Fail Open vs Fail Closed".

Why this lesson

You already run a fail-closed operation: if a KPI is missing, if an agent's adherence data is absent, if a forecast input is incomplete — you do not guess, you hold the decision. The software pattern is the same instinct, formalised. This lesson maps your ops reflex onto the engineering term so it stops feeling foreign.

Concept

A fail-closed system, on missing input or missing authority, refuses to act rather than proceeding with a default. The opposite, fail-open, proceeds with a permissive default — faster, and usually wrong in safety-critical paths.

Three properties follow:

  • Deny by default. A permission or classification not explicitly granted is treated as denied. Helix's RBAC "defaults to deny".
  • No implicit authority. The system does not inherit trust from context; the acting role must carry the permission for the specific engine and data classification.
  • Record the refusal. A held or dead-lettered submission is written to the audit ledger before any execution attempt, so the refusal is replayable.

Helix's implementation (from your repo)

The gate in control_plane/governance.py evaluates four things against the acting role's profile: estimated financial cost vs. approval limit; data classification vs. what the role may read; confidence score; and engine ownership. It returns dead_letter, awaiting_approval, or executing — and on a miss it refuses, it does not guess. The oversight_only seats propose/review but never execute, enforced by hard-deny branches regardless of target_engine.

Notice the asymmetry of cost: a false positive (holding a valid submission) costs a retry; a false negative (executing an unauthorised action) costs a breach. Fail-closed optimises for the second.

Homework (5 minutes, no code)

Pick one decision in your ops history where you held rather than guessed (missing KPI data, incomplete onboarding cohort, unverified forecast). Write down: what was missing, who owned the decision, and what evidence would have unblocked it. That triple is exactly what a fail-closed gate records.
