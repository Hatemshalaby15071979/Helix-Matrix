Lesson 0009 — RAG confidence gate

Primary source: Lewis et al. 2020 — Retrieval-Augmented Generation + your LIVE Support Assistant design.

Why this lesson

RAG (retrieve context, then generate) is the standard way to make an LLM answer from a private corpus. The danger is overconfidence: a slightly-relevant passage becomes a hallucinated answer. A confidence gate refuses or escalates when the best retrieval is not decisively better than the runner-up.

Concept

  • Retrieval: find top-k passages by similarity (cosine/inner product over embeddings).
  • Margin: gap between best and second-best similarity. Small margin → ambiguous → don't answer.
  • Confidence gate: if margin < threshold (or best score < floor), escalate to human / refuse.
  • Refusal is a feature: "I don't know" beats a confident wrong answer.
  • Corpus integrity: refuse to publish a self-contradictory corpus; 10 verification checks gate escalation behaviour.

LIVE Support Assistant: at 48 procedures, in-scope recall 27.8% (84/302) at margin 0.18; 6 of 52 out-of-scope questions answered at margin 0.18; deployed 39-ticket parity run found 6 unsafe at margins 0.1869–0.4175. The gate is the honest part.
